# Literature and Research Context — Initial Review

## Core option-pricing / volatility literature
1. Black & Scholes (1973), *The Pricing of Options and Corporate Liabilities*, Journal of Political Economy. Establishes the option-pricing framework used here for the deterministic break-even approximation.
2. Carr & Wu (2009), *Variance Risk Premiums*, Review of Financial Studies 22(3), 1311–1341. Develops a framework for measuring variance risk premia from option prices and realized variance.

## India / NIFTY evidence
3. *Dynamics of variance risk premium: Evidence from India* (2020), Journal of Economics and Finance. Reports evidence on variance risk in Indian index options; the abstract reports negative returns for market-neutral Nifty straddles in the studied sample and robustness across volatility forecasts/subsamples.
4. Bhat, Pandey & Nageswara Rao (2024), *The asymmetry in day and night option returns: Evidence from an emerging market*, Journal of Futures Markets 44(8), 1320–1337. Finds a day/night asymmetry in short Nifty option returns and argues the option-selling premium is strongly related to overnight risk.
5. Hora (2025), *Does the variance risk premium (VRP) from NIFTY options drive excess returns in a volatility-selling strategy?* Reports a positive variance risk premium in a five-year NIFTY/India VIX sample; this is a recent journal publication and should be treated as contextual evidence rather than proof of this specific calendar strategy.
6. Agarwal (2026), *The Variance Risk Premium in Nifty 50: A Structural Anatomy Across Nine Empirical Filters* (SSRN preprint). Uses one-minute NIFTY option bars and examines VRP persistence, inversion and tail asymmetry. This is a preprint and is not treated as peer-reviewed evidence.

## Calendar-spread-specific evidence
7. Schneider & Tavin (2014), *From the Samuelson Volatility Effect to a Samuelson Correlation Effect: Evidence from Crude Oil Calendar Spread Options*. Studies volatility/term-structure and dependence effects specific to calendar-spread pricing in another asset class.
8. Tang, Agrawal & Tachona (2026), *Call Calendar Spread on Overnight Option Strategy* (SSRN). Studies a calendar-spread strategy on SPY using historical option data; useful for mechanism and research-design context, not direct evidence for NIFTY.

## Research implication
The relevant literature supports three separate ideas that must not be conflated: option pricing/Greeks, variance-risk/volatility premia, and calendar-spread term-structure effects. A positive result for one does not establish profitability for the complete NIFTY double-calendar rule set. The next phase should therefore isolate the monthly and bi-weekly variants and test whether performance depends on front/back IV relationship, realized volatility, overnight gaps, and market-regime state.
